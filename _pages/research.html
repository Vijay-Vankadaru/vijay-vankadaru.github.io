One question runs through all of this work: when a model already holds the right information internally, what makes its output contradict it?

The interpretability projects ask it of language and vision-language models directly. The clinical projects, at UCSF and the University of Maryland School of Medicine, ask it where the cost of a confident wrong answer is a patient: a glioma classifier that fails for younger patients, or a cancer-typing model whose representation decides what it can and cannot see.
